Featured Poem

The Turning

Autumn

When amber steals the maple's green

and morning fog forgets to lift,

I find you in the space between

the holding on and letting drift.

The goldenrod has caught the light

that summer left without goodbye—

a reckless, beautiful mistake

now gold against the pewter sky.

I've learned to love the threshold hours,

the neither-warm-nor-cold of dusk,

when the year sheds its borrowed powers

and stands revealed in bark and husk.

So let the starlings wheel and call

their ancient code above the field.

Every beginning needs a fall.

Every wound needs time to yield.

— QuillVerse Editorial
